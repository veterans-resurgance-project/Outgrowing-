# AFTI Field Operations SOP: Mobile Biochar Production
## Workflow Protocol & Step-by-Step Deployment Guide

### I. Personnel & Role Breakdown
An efficient 2-person or 3-person field team ensures continuous, safe production without bottlenecks between processing, burning, and bagging.

1. **Lead Operator / Burn Master (1 FTE)**
   * **Responsibilities:** Oversees site safety, manages kiln ignition, regulates draft and burn temperatures, monitors complete carbonization (avoiding complete ash conversion), and directs water-quenching operations.

2. **Material Handler / Loader (1-2 FTE)**
   * **Responsibilities:** Manages feedstock sizing, feeds the kiln consistently to maintain thermal mass, handles the quenching water supply, and executes final super-sack filling, weighing, and staging.

---

### II. Step-by-Step Field Deployment & Production Workflow

#### Phase 1: Site Arrival & Setup (Approx. 30–45 Minutes)
* **Positioning & Leveling:** Back the mobile trailer into the designated staging footprint. Chock wheels, drop stabilizing jacks on solid timber blocking, and ensure the trailer deck is level.
* **Safety Perimeter & Fire Break:** Clear a 15-foot radius of dry duff, leaves, and surface debris around the trailer. Establish wind direction.
* **Utility & Quenching Readiness:** Unload the water IBC tote or pressure tanks. Position fire extinguishers, backpack pumps, and hand tools (shovels, rakes) in the designated safety zone.
* **Feedstock Staging:** Unload and stack pre-sorted dry slash or wood chips within arm's reach of the kiln feed door, keeping them segregated by size class (2-inch vs. 4-inch material).

#### Phase 2: Ignition & Continuous Feeding (Approx. 2–4 Hours per Batch)
* **Kindling & Draft Setup:** Initialize the fire using dry kindling inside the kiln chamber to establish an initial oxygen-starved draft.
* **Gradual Fuel Upscaling:** Once a stable thermal core is established, introduce larger split wood or forest slash progressively.
* **Thermal Regulation (The Loader's Role):** Feed material at a steady, measured pace rather than stuffing the chamber. Avoid smothering the fire completely or opening vents too wide, which causes runaway combustion and turns char into white ash.
* **Smoke Monitoring:** Watch exhaust color. Transitioning from white/gray steam (moisture driving off) to translucent blue-gray gas indicates optimal pyrolysis temperatures (400°C to 600°C).

#### Phase 3: Quenching & Cooling (Approx. 30–60 Minutes)
* **Termination of Air Supply:** Once the chamber is packed with fully carbonized material, seal all primary air intakes completely to starve the fire of oxygen.
* **Thermal Quenching:** Apply water via a fine spray mist or metered injection directly into the chamber (or dump into a dedicated quench trough, depending on your specific kiln design). *Caution: Wear proper respiratory PPE (P100) and eye protection during steam generation.*
* **Temperature Verification:** Ensure internal core temperatures drop below ambient safety thresholds before handling. The char must be completely black, structurally intact, and cold to the touch to prevent spontaneous combustion in storage bags.

#### Phase 4: Inoculation, Bagging & Site Wrap-Up (Approx. 45 Minutes)
* **Nutrient Charging (Optional Field Step):** If charging on-site, blend raw biochar with liquid compost tea, mycorrhizal inoculants, or mineral dusts in a mixing tub or directly as it funnels into the bag.
* **Super-Sack Loading:** Position a 1,000–1,500 lb agricultural super-sack beneath the discharge chute or use a wide-mouth shovel/funnel system. Fill evenly to avoid shifting loads during transport.
* **Securing & Staging:** Cinch and tie off the super-sack top. Use tractor forks or a pickup tailgate lifter to transfer the bag to the finished product staging zone.
* **Site Policing:** Sweep the footprint, pack out all tools, secure trailer tie-downs, and verify zero active embers before departing the landing.
