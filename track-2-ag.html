Stage 1

High-Tunnel Microclimate & Soil Texture Mechanics

High-tunnel cultivation extends the growing season via passive solar gain, but requires active management to prevent extreme temperature spikes and fungal growth. Concurrently, evaluating soil texture through physical field methods dictates optimal water retention and nutrient amendment strategies.

Thermal Mass & Cross-Ventilation

Integrating water barrels or soil berms inside the perimeter absorbs excess daytime heat. Roll-up side walls must be adjusted based on wind direction and relative humidity to maintain vapor pressure deficit (VPD).

The Ribbon Test Field Method

Moisten soil and press it into a ribbon between thumb and forefinger. Sands crumble instantly, loams form fragile ribbons under 1 inch, and clays form long, flexible ribbons indicating high cation exchange capacity (CEC).

Stage 2

Irrigation Engineering & Resource Guides

Precision micro-irrigation layout requires proper filtration, pressure regulation, and layout geometry to ensure uniform water distribution across crop rows.

Core Installation Requirements:

  1. Filtration & Regulation: Always install a 150-mesh screen filter and a 25 PSI pressure regulator immediately downstream from your water source to prevent emitter clogging and line rupture.
  2. Drip Tape Layout: Unroll pressure-compensating emitter tubing along crop rows with emitters facing upward to prevent sediment settling. Secure with ground staples every 4 feet.

Stage 3

Action Steps & Field Construction Protocols

Step-by-step assembly sequences for testing and pressurizing sub-main manifold lines.

Step 1: Flushing Mainlines

Before connecting drip tape or micro-sprinklers, open all mainline end caps and flush the system at full volume for 2 minutes to clear out PVC shavings, dirt, and pipe cement residue.

Step 2: Pressure Testing

Cap line ends, engage water supply gradually, and inspect all barbed fittings and compression couplers for leaks before backfilling subterranean lines or mulching over surface runs.

Understanding the ratio of sand, silt, and clay. Field testing via ribbon tests and jar settling methods to evaluate drainage rates, cation exchange capacity (CEC), and organic matter content.
