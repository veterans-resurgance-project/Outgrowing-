Staff Training Manual: USDA CIG

1. What This Grant Is & Why We Use It

The USDA Conservation Innovation Grants (CIG) fund the development and demonstration of innovative, field-tested conservation practices, data collection protocols, and agricultural technology. For AFTI, this grant supports our standardized telemetry data pipeline (Module 203) and our integration with university and academic extension partners.

2. How the Operating Model Works in Practice

  • Rigorous Field Data Collection: Recruits and stewards log real-time soil health, moisture, and environmental metrics using standardized JSON/CSV formats with UTC timestamps and plot ID metadata.
  • Extension Feedback Loops: Clean, verified data is fed directly to research partners, turning working cooperative plots into active "living laboratory" research sites.
  • Quality Assurance: Built-in metadata rules prevent data drift, ensuring field telemetry is audit-ready for scientific analysis.

3. Step-by-Step Staff Operating Guide

  • Step 1 (Document Telemetry Schemas): Export system documentation detailing our Module 203 data standards, sensor calibration rules, and observer verification routines.
  • Step 2 (Secure Extension Letters): Attach formal partner agreements (e.g., OSU Extension or regional research institutions) receiving our field telemetry datasets.
  • Step 3 (Define Environmental KPIs): List specific target benchmarks (soil organic matter tracking, water usage efficiency, carbon offset modeling) measured across plots.
  • Step 4 (Finalize Technical Budget): Outline funding requests for field sensors, telemetry hardware, database cloud infrastructure, and research reporting staff.
