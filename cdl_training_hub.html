The Complete Path to Getting Your CDL

Acquiring a Commercial Driver's License involves specific federal and state milestones. Follow this chronological blueprint from medical qualification to your final on-road driving exam.

1

Self-Certification & DOT Medical Card

Prerequisite

Before scheduling any DMV tests, you must self-certify your operating status (Interstate vs. Intrastate) and undergo a physical examination by a registered Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) Medical Examiner.

Key Requirements:
  • Vision check (20/40 in each eye with or without correction).
  • Blood pressure control and physical stamina assessment.
  • Filing Medical Examiner's Certificate (Form MCSA-5876) with your state licensing agency.
2

DMV Knowledge Tests & Commercial Learner's Permit (CLP)

Written Exam

Pass the written general knowledge and specialized endorsement tests at your local DMV to earn your Commercial Learner’s Permit (CLP). You must hold this permit for a minimum of 14 days before attempting any skills testing.

General Knowledge 50 Questions (80% passing score required for all Class A/B candidates).
Air Brakes 25 Questions (Mandatory if driving vehicles equipped with air brake systems).
Combination Vehicles 20 Questions (Required for Class A tractor-trailer combinations).
3

Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT)

Federal Mandatory

Under federal regulations, all first-time CDL applicants must complete theory and behind-the-wheel instruction through an FMCSA-registered training provider listed on the Training Provider Registry (TPR).

Note: Your training results must be submitted electronically by your certified instructor to the TPR before you can book your final road exam.

4

Pre-Trip Vehicle Inspection Test

Practical Phase 1

You must demonstrate to the examiner that your commercial vehicle is safe to operate. You will conduct a full walk-around inspection, pointing out key mechanical components, checking fluid levels, inspect brake lines, and execute the crucial 7-Step Air Brake Leakage Test.

5

Basic Vehicle Control Skills

Practical Phase 2

Test your spatial awareness and vehicle control through a series of yard maneuvers around cones. Maneuvers typically include straight-line backing, offset backing, alley docking, and parallel parking.

6

On-Road Driving Examination

Final Phase

Drive on public roadways with a state examiner evaluating your performance in real traffic conditions. You will be graded on turns, intersections, railroad crossings, gear shifting/clutch work, lane positioning, mirror checks, and overall safety etiquette.
