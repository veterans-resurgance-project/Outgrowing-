# FAA Part 107 Cohort Training | Day 2: Airspace Classification & Sectional Charts * **Target Audience:** Veteran Cohort (Hillside Remediation & Agricultural Missions) * **Total Time:** 4.5 Hours (2.5 hr morning block, 2 hr afternoon practical chart-reading workshop) --- ## I. Core Lecture Modules ### Module 1: The National Airspace System (NAS) Architecture (75 Mins) * **Controlled vs. Uncontrolled Airspace:** * **Controlled Airspace (Classes B, C, D, E):** Airspace of defined dimensions within which Air Traffic Control (ATC) service is provided. Part 107 pilots require prior authorization (typically via LAANC or DroneZone) to operate here. * **Uncontrolled Airspace (Class G):** Airspace where ATC has no authority or responsibility to control air traffic. Part 107 flight is permitted up to 400 feet AGL without prior ATC authorization. * **Airspace Tiers & Dimensions:** * **Class B (Busy/Big Hubs):** Surface up to 10,000 feet MSL. Usually shaped like an upside-down wedding cake with multiple tiers. Explicit ATC clearance required. * **Class C (Medium Hubs / Radar Towers):** Surface to 4,000 feet AGL above airport elevation. Core surface area is 5 NM radius; shelf area extends to 10 NM radius (1,200 to 4,000 feet AGL). Two-way radio communication required before entering. * **Class D (Smaller Towers):** Surface to 2,500 feet AGL surrounding airports with an operational control tower. Two-way radio communication required. * **Class E (Everywhere Else Controlled):** Generally begins at 700 feet AGL or 1,200 feet AGL (designated by fuzzy magenta/blue vignettes on charts) and extends up to, but not including, 18,000 feet MSL. Can also start at the surface (dashed magenta line). * **Class G (Uncontrolled):** Surface up to either 700 feet or 1,200 feet AGL, depending on the overlying Class E floor. ### Module 2: Sectional Chart Decoding & Obstacles (75 Mins) * **Reading Sectional Symbols:** * **Latitude and Longitude Ticks:** Every tick mark represents 1 minute of arc; numbers along parallels and meridians denote degrees. * **Airport Symbology:** Magenta circles indicate airports with non-hard-surfaced runways (or private airports); blue circles indicate airports with hard-surfaced runways. * **Open vs. Enclosed Circles:** Open circles mean no control tower; segmented/solid blue or magenta hash rings indicate controlled airspace structures. * **Obstacle Elevation Figures (MSL vs. AGL):** * **Maximum Elevation Figures (MEF):** Large numbers printed in each quadrangle showing the highest known feature (terrain or obstacle) in that block, rounded up to the nearest 100 feet. * **Obstacle Icons:** Tall towers and structures display their height in feet Above Ground Level (AGL) in parentheses right next to their height in Mean Sea Level (MSL). * **Special Use Airspace (SUA) & Hazards:** * **MOAs (Military Operations Areas):** Gray hashed lines; exercise extreme caution, though legal to fly through without permission. * **Restricted / Prohibited Areas:** Solid blue/magenta hash lines; flying inside is strictly prohibited without authorization. * **TFRs (Temporary Flight Restrictions):** Issued via NOTAMs for VIP movements, natural disasters, or active wildfires (critical for our hillside remediation cohort!). ### Module 3: LAANC, Authorization & Fire TFR Workflows (45 Mins) * **Automated Low Altitude Authorization and Notification Capability (LAANC):** * Utilizing approved apps (Aloft, AutoPilot, etc.) to secure near-instantaneous low-altitude authorizations near controlled agricultural airports or municipal facilities. * **Wildfire TFR Protocols:** * Active fires instantly trigger a strict TFR (typically 2,000 feet AGL and a 5 nautical mile radius minimum around the incident). * **Contextual Application:** Hillside remediation missions must check for active local fire suppression activity and NOTAMs before every deployment. Violating a wildfire TFR carries massive federal fines and risks grounding life-saving firefighting aircraft. --- ## II. End-of-Day Diagnostic Pretest ### Question 1: Airspace Classification You are setting up an agricultural drone flight over a rural field located directly beneath a segmented blue dashed circle surrounding a small regional airport with an active control tower. What class of airspace is this surface area? * A) Class G uncontrolled airspace. * B) Class D controlled airspace. * C) Class C airspace. > **Correct Answer: B** > *Explanation:* A dashed blue circle around an airport with a control tower denotes Class D airspace starting at the surface. ### Question 2: Sectional Chart Obstacles On a VFR sectional chart, you notice an obstacle symbol with the number **1249** printed above **(452)**. What do these numbers represent? * A) 1,249 feet MSL (height of the top of the structure) and 452 feet AGL (height of the structure from the ground). * B) 1,249 feet AGL and 452 feet MSL. * C) The structure is 1,249 feet wide and stands 452 feet tall. > **Correct Answer: A** > *Explanation:* The top number is the height in Mean Sea Level (MSL), and the parenthetical number is the height Above Ground Level (AGL). ### Question 3: TFRs and Emergency Operations While preparing to map a remote Willamette Valley hillside, you pull up pre-flight NOTAMs and discover an active TFR over the ridge due to an ongoing forestry management burn and aerial suppression effort. Can you fly your drone inside this TFR? * A) Yes, as long as you stay under 400 feet AGL and maintain visual line of sight. * B) No, unless you are participating in the official relief/firefighting operation or have received explicit ATC/Incident Commander waiver authorization. * C) Yes, provided the drone weighs less than 55 pounds. > **Correct Answer: B** > *Explanation:* TFRs prohibit unauthorized aircraft—manned or unmanned—from entering the restricted zone to ensure safety for emergency response and firefighting aircraft.
