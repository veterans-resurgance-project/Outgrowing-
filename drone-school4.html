# FAA Part 107 Cohort Training | Day 4: Aircraft Loading, Performance, Emergencies & CRM * **Target Audience:** Veteran Cohort (Hillside Remediation & Agricultural Missions) * **Total Time:** 4.5 Hours (2.5 hr morning block, 2 hr afternoon practical emergency simulation workshop) --- ## I. Core Lecture Modules ### Module 1: Weight, Balance & Payload Physics (75 Mins) * **Center of Gravity (CG) Limits:** * Importance of balanced payload distribution. An improperly balanced drone (heavy front/rear or off-center pods) forces flight controllers to overcompensate with individual motor adjustments, drastically reducing stability and battery life. * **Agricultural & Remediation Payload Dynamics:** * **Liquid Spray Systems:** Fluid shifting dynamics inside agricultural tanks cause sudden weight redistribution during rapid turns, climbs, or descents on hillsides. * **Multispectral & Sensor Pods:** Accounting for extra weight, power draw, and aerodynamic drag when mounting specialized cameras or LiDAR hardware. * **Battery Chemistry & Lithium-Polymer (LiPo) Safety:** * Voltage sag under heavy loads, storage charging protocols, safe discharge rates (C-ratings), and thermal runaway risks during high-stress operations. ### Module 2: Emergency Procedures & Contingency Planning (75 Mins) * **In-Flight System Failures:** * **Lost Link (RTH):** Configuring failsafe Return-to-Home altitude parameters correctly to clear local tree lines, terrain ridges, and power lines on hillsides. * **Fly-Away Scenarios:** Identifying compass/GPS interference, magnetic anomalies (common near iron-rich soils or metal infrastructure), and executing manual override procedures. * **Low Battery / Critical Voltage Warnings:** Establishing hard rules for bingo fuel (battery percentage required to return safely against head-winds). * **Collision Avoidance & Immediate Evasion:** * Quick-reaction maneuvers when encountering unannounced manned aircraft, crop dusters, or local wildlife (raptors and birds of prey). ### Module 3: Crew Resource Management (CRM) & Mission Coordination (45 Mins) * **Defining Roles on the Team:** * **Remote Pilot in Command (RPIC):** Maintains final legal and operational control, monitors flight telemetry, and manages emergency responses. * **Visual Observer (VO):** Acts as the pilot's eyes, scanning blind spots, monitoring traffic, maintaining VLOS compliance, and watching out for terrain hazards. * **Effective Communication Protocols:** * Standardizing callouts (e.g., "Traffic 2 o'clock low," "Battery at 30%," "Visual contact lost"). Eliminating ambiguity during high-stress missions like active hillside monitoring or multi-acre crop spraying. --- ## II. End-of-Day Diagnostic Pretest ### Question 1: Weight and Balance When operating a multirotor drone equipped with an interchangeable agricultural liquid spray tank, how does a half-full, unbaffled liquid load affect aircraft handling during sharp maneuvers on a steep slope? * A) It has no effect because liquids automatically center themselves via centrifugal force. * B) Liquid sloshing shifts the center of gravity dynamically, causing unexpected attitude corrections and increased power demand. * C) It improves stability by lowering the overall center of gravity. > **Correct Answer: B** > *Explanation:* Unbaffled liquid shifting creates dynamic weight distribution changes, which can destabilize the aircraft and make handling unpredictable during aggressive hillside maneuvers. ### Question 2: Emergency Failsafes You are operating a mapping drone over a rugged ridge line and suddenly experience a complete loss of control link between your controller and the aircraft. What should you expect the drone to do by default if properly configured? * A) Immediately cut power and drop straight down. * B) Execute a pre-programmed Return-to-Home (RTH) sequence, climbing to a designated altitude that clears local obstacles and returning to the launch point. * C) Hover in place indefinitely until the battery dies. > **Correct Answer: B** > *Explanation:* A properly configured failsafe triggers an automated RTH sequence, ascending to a safe altitude to clear terrain before navigating back to the home point. ### Question 3: Crew Resource Management During an agricultural spraying run, your designated Visual Observer (VO) spots a crop duster approaching fast from behind a tree line at low altitude, but the RPIC is heads-down verifying telemetry. What is the correct communication protocol? * A) Wait until the current grid pass is finished before interrupting the pilot. * B) Immediately issue a clear, concise verbal alert stating the threat direction and relative altitude, prompting the RPIC to take evasive action. * C) Take the controller away from the pilot physically. > **Correct Answer: B** > *Explanation:* Clear, immediate verbal callouts from the VO are a cornerstone of Crew Resource Management, enabling the RPIC to instantly yield right-of-way and execute evasive maneuvers.
