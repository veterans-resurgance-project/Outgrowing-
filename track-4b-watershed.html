Stage 1

Post-Wildfire Soil Mechanics & Erosion Mitigation

High-intensity wildfires bake organic compounds into the soil, creating a waxy, water-repellent crust. This hydrophobic layer stops normal rainwater infiltration, turning light showers into rapid, high-velocity surface runoff that threatens local aquatic habitats and property.

Contour Wattling Installation

Crews bundle certified weed-free straw or brush into cylindrical tubes. These must be staked horizontally along true slope contours—never straight up and down—using wooden live stakes to break surface flow velocity.

Log Check Dams

Installed in minor gullies and ephemeral draws. Small-diameter logs are embedded into the trench floor and sides, secured with stakes, to capture migrating sediment and flatten the hydraulic grade line.

Stage 2

Hazardous Fuels Reduction & Defensible Space

Systematic thinning of vertical ladder fuels and horizontal fuel continuity reduces fire intensity and protects fragile watershed ecosystems from catastrophic crown fires.

Core Fuel Reduction Standards:

  1. Ladder Fuel Pruning: Eliminates continuous vertical fuel profiles (low-hanging tree branches, dense shrubs under mature canopies) that allow ground fires to climb into forest crowns. Target a vertical clearance standard of 2 to 3 times the height of ground vegetation.
  2. Slash Piling & Management: Remnant debris is consolidated into compact, wildlife-conscious slash piles outside of drip lines and drainage paths, designated for controlled burning or chipping operations.

Stage 3

Action Steps & Field Construction Protocols

Step-by-step deployment sequences for constructing sediment barriers on unstable slopes.

Step 1: Trenching on Contour

Use a hand level or clinometer to map true contour lines. Excavate a shallow trench 2 to 4 inches deep to anchor straw wattles securely against seepage underneath.

Step 2: Staking & Overlap

Drive wooden stakes every 3 to 4 feet straight through the center of the wattle. Ensure adjacent wattle ends are tightly abutted—never overlapped—to prevent water channeling between joints.
