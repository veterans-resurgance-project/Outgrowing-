SOP #04 • Volunteer Training Module

Broadcast Seeding & Burn Scar Rehabilitation

Target Window: October 1 – November 30 (Pre-Winter Rain Timing)

Volunteer XP +100 XP

Event Overview & Objective

Seeding right before late-autumn rains ensures seeds experience natural winter cold stratification while preventing bird predation. Establishing native root webs on ash slopes stabilizes soil and prevents severe erosion during winter rainstorms.

Field Prep Focus

  • Target moderate-to-high burn severity zones
  • Rake ash crust to establish direct seed-to-soil contact
  • Avoid seeding over hydrophobic (water-repellent) ash cap

Required Tools & PPE

  • Hand-crank broadcast seeders & buckets
  • McLeod tools, bow rakes & hand cultivators
  • Weed-free straw bales for mulching
  • Dust masks (N95) for dry ash areas

Step-by-Step Seeding & Mulching Protocol

1. Surface Preparation (Scuffing) Lightly rake or scuff hard-baked soil or ash crust using McLeod tools or bow rakes to create 1/4-inch shallow grooves for seed retention.
2. Calibrated Broadcast Seeding Walk parallel along slope contours. Broadcast native seed mix evenly using hand-crank seeders at the prescribed rate (e.g., 15–20 lbs per acre). Cast half the mix in one direction and the remaining half perpendicular to ensure coverage.
3. Pressing Seed-to-Soil Contact Lightly rake or walk over seeded soil. Seeds resting on top of loose ash without direct soil contact will dry out and die.
4. Certified Weed-Free Straw Mulching Spread a light layer of weed-free straw (1 to 2 inches thick) over seeded zones. Mulch retains soil moisture and buffers rain impact while allowing sunlight penetration.
